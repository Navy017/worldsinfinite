# World Premises — Power Systems & Magic

Research catalogue for Worlds Infinite's **World Premise** layer: the 1–2 hidden backbone ideas that make a generated world feel authored. This file covers **power systems and magic premises** drawn from anime/manga/shonen, Western fantasy, games and web-fiction.

**Naming rule.** Every premise has an *original* Worlds Infinite name and original in-world vocabulary. The "Inspirations" line names source works **for our reference only**; nothing in that line (character names, trademarked terms such as specific power names) may appear in generated output. All other fields use our own terms.

**Count.** 23 premises after merging near-duplicates (merge notes are listed under each premise).

---

## How to read an entry

| Field | Meaning for the generator |
|---|---|
| **Pitch** | One line shown to designers / used in seed summaries. |
| **Core truth** | The hidden fact players piece together from fragments. Pick **one** option per world (options are mutually exclusive variants). |
| **Rules** | Source, cost, limits, taboo, acquisition. These drive NPC abilities, faction law and event hooks. |
| **Wielders** | Institutions, orders, bloodlines and outlaws to spawn as factions. |
| **Subthemes** | Regional/story arcs. A region typically adopts 1–2 of these. |
| **Mysteries** | Dig sites, ruins and anomalies to place on the map. |
| **Adds** | Creatures, people types and items to inject into tables. |
| **Progression** | Knowledge/tech ladder from early to late; can be used to stage eras or to tie discoveries to civilization tech level. |
| **Map effects** | Visible terrain/settlement changes for the renderer. |
| **Fragments** | Example lore sources and their bias — what each source gets wrong or hides. |
| **Tags** | Compatibility metadata (see vocabulary below). |

### Tag vocabulary

**Slots** (a world should rarely have two premises filling the same *exclusive* slot; marked *):
- `magic-source`* — where power ultimately comes from.
- `power-system-personal` — innate/trained power inside individuals.
- `power-system-external` — power stored in items, substances, beings or contracts.
- `cosmology`* — what the world/universe fundamentally is.
- `apocalypse` — a looming or cyclic end.
- `world-edge` — what lies beyond / beneath / outside (other realms, the void, the sea).
- `state-control` — a premise that naturally produces a monopoly, registry or hunting of wielders.
- `mind-substrate` — consciousness, emotion, dreams or belief have physical effect.
- `bloodline` — heredity matters to power.
- `divine-order` — gods, patrons or a cosmic law-giver are central.
- `economy-resource` — power depends on a mined/harvested/traded commodity.
- `monster-source` — the premise explains where monsters come from.

**Tone:** heroic, grim, whimsical, horror, mythic, (plus occasional: melancholic, political, adventurous).
**Era range:** stone, bronze, medieval, renaissance, industrial, modern, future.
**Scale:** personal, regional, world.

## 1. The Balanced Circle (Scalecraft)

**Pitch:** A rigorous science of transmutation where nothing is made from nothing — and the greatest works of the age are secretly paid for in human lives.

**Inspirations:** Fullmetal Alchemist (equivalent exchange, Philosopher's Stone, Amestris/Xing alkahestry), historical alchemy (Hermeticism, the Magnum Opus, nigredo/albedo/rubedo), Atelier series (synthesis), Kingkiller Chronicle (conservation of energy as magic law).

**Core truth (choose one):**
1. **The Red Catalyst is condensed souls.** The legendary stone that "breaks the law of balance" doesn't break it at all — it is a reservoir of human lives, and every miracle draws them down.
2. **The nation is the circle.** The borders, roads and fortresses of the founding kingdom were laid out as one continent-sized transmutation array; its wars and massacres were placed at nodes to charge it. The founders are waiting for the final activation.
3. **The flow is not ours.** The energy for transmutation doesn't come from the earth's crust as taught — it is siphoned from a sleeping thing beneath the world (or from a neighbouring land, which is slowly dying).

**Rules:**
- **Source:** Material equivalence plus an energy flow (taught: tectonic "earth-current"; truth varies).
- **Cost:** Mass and kind must balance — iron becomes iron, never gold; complexity costs study, not fuel. Failed work rebounds on the body (lost limbs, organs).
- **Limits:** Cannot create from nothing, cannot make gold (often legally forbidden anyway), cannot restore a soul once lost. Needs an array (drawn circle) unless the practitioner has "seen the far side".
- **Taboo:** Human transmutation — remaking or resurrecting a person. Those who attempt it pay with body parts and gain forbidden insight (circle-less casting).
- **Acquisition:** Trained (academies, apprenticeships); some lineages have inherited research notes encrypted as cookbooks or herbals.

**Wielders:**
- **State Circle-Wrights** — licensed, ranked, salaried weapons of the crown ("dogs of the throne").
- **The Eastern Flow-Readers** — a foreign school that reads the earth-current for medicine and distance-work; considered primitive by the state school.
- **Hedge-wrights** — rural menders of ploughs and wells; unlicensed but tolerated.
- **The Stone-seekers** — a secret society, often inside the government.
- **Circle-breakers** — zealots who hunt wrights as blasphemers who "unmake God's forms".

**Subthemes:**
- **Chimera regiments:** an army of fused beast-soldiers, discarded after the war, now feral or seeking rights.
- **The sacrificed province:** a region purged in a "rebellion" that was really an array node charge.
- **The quest for the Red Catalyst:** relic-hunting across ruins, each fake stone costing a village.
- **The lost eastern technique:** the foreign Flow-Readers can work where the state school fails — why?
- **Zealot inquisition:** a religious state that burns wrights, unaware its own temple miracles are transmutation.
- **The broken siblings:** a family undone by an attempted resurrection; their failed creation still lives.
- **Gold ban economics:** a counterfeiting crisis when someone finally makes gold — and the cost is revealed.
- **Homunculus court:** artificial beings with names of vices quietly staffing the ministries.
- **The desert city that vanished overnight:** an ancient precedent of a national-scale transmutation.
- **Prosthetic guilds:** steel-limb engineers flourishing among the maimed.
- **The licensing exam:** a yearly tournament that doubles as recruitment for a war.

**Mysteries / dig sites:**
- A ruined city whose streets form a perfect circle visible only from a mountaintop; everyone died the same night.
- A sealed laboratory with a skeleton missing exactly the limbs that a failed transmutation would take.
- Underground tunnels tracing a ring around the capital, with blood-stained node chambers.
- A "flowless" valley where no transmutation works — the current has been cut (or redirected).
- A stone tablet in two scripts: the state circle and the eastern ring, describing the same act as "theft" and "gift".
- Red crystal shards in a mass grave, each warm to the touch.

**Adds:**
- Chimeras (human-beast fusions; intelligent, bitter).
- Homunculi (ageless artificial beings with regeneration, each with a fatal flaw).
- Armour-bound souls (a soul fixed to a suit of armour with a blood sigil).
- Circle-gloves / tattooed arrays (instant-cast items).
- Steel-limb prosthetics and their engineers.
- Red Catalyst shards (contraband, sought by every faction).
- Gate-touched wrights (circle-less casters who paid a price).

**Progression:**
1. Proto-chemistry: dyes, metallurgy, salt-working; circles as ritual.
2. Codified balance: academies, the law of equivalence, licensed practice.
3. Applied transmutation: battlefield wrights, instant fortifications, bridges raised in a night.
4. Bio-transmutation: chimeras, medicine, prosthetic fusion.
5. Catalyst era: soul-batteries, continental arrays.
6. Post-revelation: ethical "flow" medicine or a collapse into catalyst warfare.

**Map effects:**
- Geometric scars: perfectly circular craters, polygonal rivers, hexagonal ruins.
- Fortified "node towns" placed with suspicious regularity across a kingdom.
- Glass deserts where a nation-scale work went off.
- Eastern medicine-towns with strange stone pillars marking current lines.

**Fragments:**
- **State Academy textbook** — rigorous but silent on catalysts; claims transmutation is "clean".
- **A deserter's diary from the purge** — honest about atrocities, wrong about why (thinks it was ethnic hatred).
- **Eastern medicine scroll** — describes the flow as alive and grieving; dismissed as superstition.
- **Encrypted cookbook** — a family's catalyst research hidden in recipes; looks mundane.
- **Zealot sermon** — calls wrights soul-thieves; theologically wrong, accidentally correct.

**Tags:** slots `magic-source`, `power-system-personal`, `state-control`, `economy-resource` (catalyst) · tone grim, heroic · era renaissance–industrial (works medieval) · scale regional→world.

## 2. Vowfire

**Pitch:** Everyone has a life-aura; a trained few can shape it — and the deadliest are those who bind themselves with vows that would kill them if broken.

**Inspirations:** Hunter x Hunter (Nen: aura types, conditions & vows, posthumous Nen), Jujutsu Kaisen (binding vows), One Piece (Haki: willpower as armour/foresight/king's will), Dragon Ball (ki), martial-arts wuxia (internal energy), Gurren Lagann (fighting spirit).

**Core truth (choose one):**
1. **Vows outlive their makers.** The aura of a person who dies with an unfulfilled vow persists and becomes *stronger*; much of the world's "haunting" is old vows still trying to complete themselves. A long-dead oath is the root of the current war.
2. **The six temperaments were imposed.** The classification of aura into six temperaments (and the test used to sort children) is a political tool of the founding order; a seventh, suppressed temperament exists and is born only in a hunted people.
3. **The world's own vow.** The continent itself is bound by a vow made by its first people — the boundaries of the known world are a restriction that grants the land its power. Breaking out of the known map breaks the vow.

**Rules:**
- **Source:** One's own life-aura; quality tied to resolve.
- **Cost:** Stamina and life-force; overuse ages the user.
- **Limits:** Every person has one temperament with falloff in others; abilities must fit temperament and imagination.
- **Vow mechanic:** Self-imposed restrictions (only usable against one enemy, only after declaring the technique, only at night) and penalties (lose the power, lose a limb, die) multiply strength. Mutual vows between two people bind both.
- **Taboo:** Forcing someone's aura open by violence (kills most); feeding aura with others' lives; breaking a vow (backlash).
- **Acquisition:** Trained (slow meditation) or forced (violent "awakening"); sometimes awakened by trauma.

**Wielders:**
- **The Licensed Seekers** — a guild of elite adventurer-hunters with a brutal entrance exam.
- **Aura monasteries** — teach the slow, safe opening; keep the temperament test.
- **Arena circuits** — towers of combat where fighters climb floors for money; the gateway to aura for commoners.
- **Crime families with "condition-bound" enforcers.**
- **The Wardbound** — a royal guard who all swore the same vow; they cannot lie to the throne.
- **Vow-lawyers** — scribes who draft and witness vows like contracts.

**Subthemes:**
- **The Tower Arena:** a city built around a 200-floor fight tower; corruption at the upper floors.
- **The grudge-weapon trade:** items imbued with the aura of their murdered makers, cursed and powerful.
- **The last of the eye-clan:** a massacred people whose eyes are traded as treasures; a survivor with a terrible vow.
- **The Seeker trial:** a yearly lethal trial; the rookie class of this year will define the age.
- **Royal succession war:** princes with aura-guardians fight aboard a vast ship headed for the unknown.
- **The seventh temperament:** a hunted child who fits no category.
- **Insectile invasion:** a new apex species learns aura instinctively and begins to farm humans.
- **The vow-debt city:** a town that collectively vowed never to bear arms — and gained invulnerability within its walls.
- **Exiles of the known world:** expeditions beyond the edge where aura is foreign and dangerous.
- **Posthumous curses:** cleansing old vows becomes a profession.

**Mysteries / dig sites:**
- A tomb where a dead master's vow still binds trespassers to fight him.
- A sealed "game" world: a whole island where an aura-construct turns life into card-based rules.
- The temperament stone that glows a colour not in any textbook.
- A ruined arena whose spectator stands are carved with thousands of vow-texts.
- A ship graveyard at the world's edge where every captain's log ends with the same promise.

**Adds:**
- Condition-bound assassins; vow-bound bodyguards.
- Cursed heirloom weapons (posthumous aura).
- Aura-born constructs (summoned bodies, puppet dolls, rule-worlds).
- Chimeric insect-kin who learn aura natively.
- Exorcists who "unbind" old vows.
- Temperament test kits (water glass with leaf — our version: a bowl of salt, a feather, a flame).

**Progression:**
1. Instinctive will: warriors' "fighting spirit", unexplained feats.
2. Monastic opening: basic control, sensing, armour-of-will.
3. Systematic temperaments, standardized test, guild licensing.
4. Vow law: formalized contracts, vow-lawyers, vows in diplomacy and commerce.
5. Constructed worlds: masters create persistent rule-spaces and artefacts.
6. Edge expeditions: encountering aura systems from beyond the known map.

**Map effects:**
- Combat towers dominating city skylines.
- "Vow zones": regions with odd, enforced laws (no fire, no metal, no lies) that work like magic borders.
- Haunted battlefields marked as no-go zones.

**Fragments:**
- **Guild exam handbook** — practical, omits that some examinees are expected to die.
- **Monastery primer** — moralistic: says vows are vanity; actually afraid of them.
- **Arena tabloid** — sensationalized fighter profiles, accurate about techniques by accident.
- **A vow-contract** — exact text, but missing the secret second clause known only to the parties.
- **Edge expedition log** — terrified, contradicts known aura theory.

**Tags:** slots `power-system-personal`, `mind-substrate` (will) · tone heroic, grim · era medieval–modern · scale personal→regional (world if truth 3).

## 3. The Grudgewell

**Pitch:** Fear, hatred and grief leak from every human and pool into living curses; sorcerers fight them with the same poison — and the best can fold the world into the shape of their own soul.

**Inspirations:** Jujutsu Kaisen (cursed energy, cursed spirits, domain expansion, cursed techniques, binding vows), Mob Psycho 100 (spirits, psychic emotion), Chainsaw Man (devils born of fear, power scales with how feared a concept is), Natsume's Book of Friends / Mushishi (yokai and invisible life), Japanese onryō folklore.

**Core truth (choose one):**
1. **The barrier is a person.** The only reason cities aren't drowned in curses is a centuries-old immortal sealed at the heart of the capital, maintaining a ward — and he is beginning to turn into something else. His successor must be "merged" with him.
2. **Fear of concepts makes gods.** The strongest curses are born from the shared fear of an idea (death, fire, the sea, the dark). Wiping out a fear wipes out the thing from human memory — and the world's lost words are evidence of erased fears.
3. **Sorcerers are the source.** The curse-producing leak is worst near sorcerers themselves; the institutions that "protect" humanity are breeding the problem. A faction proposes to eliminate all non-sorcerers (who leak) — or all sorcerers (who amplify).

**Rules:**
- **Source:** Negative emotion leaking from humans; sorcerers can refine it rather than leak it.
- **Cost:** Stamina, sanity; "reversed" use (positive from negative) is rare and heals.
- **Limits:** Each sorcerer has an innate technique (often hereditary); others use tools or barriers. Ordinary people can't see curses.
- **Signature act:** **Self-realm** — projecting one's inner world as a sealed space where one's technique always hits. Clashing realms compete in refinement.
- **Taboo:** Eating curses (absorbs them but corrupts), using human sacrifice to seal, binding a curse into an unborn child, crossing the barrier in the capital.
- **Acquisition:** Born (innate technique), trained, vow-bound, or cursed into it (swallowing a relic).

**Wielders:**
- **The Two Schools** — eastern and western academies training young sorcerers; politically controlled by old clans.
- **The Three Houses** — ancient bloodlines with inherited techniques; nepotistic and cruel to the untalented.
- **Curse-users** — outlaw sorcerers who kill for money or ideology.
- **Shrine-keepers** — maintain small barriers at crossroads.
- **Fear-cults** — worship and feed specific curses.

**Subthemes:**
- **Relic-bones of the Cursed King:** relic parts of an ancient sorcerer-tyrant scattered and sealed; collecting them resurrects him.
- **Night of the curtain:** a festival district sealed off and turned into a massacre arena.
- **Clan succession:** a talentless heir born without energy who becomes a physical monster.
- **Disaster curses:** born from fear of earthquakes, forests, the sea — they plan to replace humanity.
- **The cursed child:** a baby used as a vessel.
- **The Harrowing Lottery:** a nationwide forced tournament run by an ancient puppeteer.
- **Hospital of grief:** an asylum whose curses are the patients' loved ones.
- **Contracts with fear-devils:** governments employing curses through vows.
- **Seeing glasses:** cheap tools let ordinary people see curses; panic and vigilantism follow.
- **The forgotten fear:** a whole concept is missing from the language, its curse eaten.

**Mysteries / dig sites:**
- A sealed reliquary holding a mummified finger that whispers.
- A town where no one remembers a colour — and nothing in it is that colour.
- An abandoned school whose gymnasium is still a perfect realm-shell.
- A ring of stone barrier posts around the capital, each etched with a dead sorcerer's name.
- A "fear map" of the country drawn by a cult, matching disaster sites.

**Adds:**
- Curses (graded by threat: grade-4 vermin to calamity-grade disasters).
- Fear-concept titans (born of fear of death, fire, darkness).
- Cursed tools (blades that cut spirits, chains of infinite length).
- Vessels (hosts of sealed tyrants), resurrected ancient sorcerers in borrowed bodies.
- Barrier-monks; window-watchers (support staff who can see but not fight).

**Progression:**
1. Spirit-folklore: offerings, charms, exorcism by ritual.
2. Codified sorcery: clans, inherited techniques, barrier shrines.
3. Academies & grading system; state contracts.
4. Self-realm mastery; realm duels as war.
5. Mass-awareness: seeing tools, public panic, curses as weapons of state.
6. Endgame: merging humanity's minds to stop the leak (or replacing humanity).

**Map effects:**
- Barrier posts and torii-like gates ringing cities.
- Curse-dense "black zones" around hospitals, battlefields, schools.
- Disaster domains: a forest, volcano or bay that is visibly alive and hostile.

**Fragments:**
- **Clan genealogy** — boasts of inherited techniques, hides bastard heirs and failures.
- **Academy grading manual** — dry, bureaucratic; underestimates disaster curses.
- **Cult hymnal** — loving descriptions of curses as children of humanity.
- **Survivor's police statement** — describes curses as "a gas leak", cover story intact.
- **The tyrant's own letter** — charismatic, lies about his goals.

**Tags:** slots `magic-source`, `power-system-personal`, `mind-substrate`, `monster-source`, `bloodline` · tone grim, horror · era medieval–modern · scale regional (world in truth 1/3).

## 4. Hungerfruit

**Pitch:** Rare fruits grant a single impossible power to whoever eats one — and the sea, which once belonged to their makers, drowns every eater who touches it.

**Inspirations:** One Piece (Devil Fruits, seastone, Logia/Paramecia/Zoan, Awakening, the Void Century), Toriko (ingredient power), Mario/folk "magic mushrooms", Fullmetal-style mystery of a hidden century, Greek myth (pomegranate of the underworld, apples of the Hesperides).

**Core truth (choose one):**
1. **The fruits are human dreams.** Each fruit is the crystallized desire of a person who wished to become something else; the first fruits grew from a dying god's wish-tree. Eating one is half-merging with that dreamer.
2. **The fruits are the vanquished.** An ancient kingdom was wiped from history; its people's powers were rendered into fruit by the victors, who still rule. The sea-curse is that kingdom's ocean refusing its killers' weapons.
3. **The fruits are a leash.** The sea-stone that negates fruit power and the fruits themselves come from the same mineral, and the world government seeded the fruit to create a dependency only it can control.

**Rules:**
- **Source:** The fruit; one power per fruit, one fruit per person (a second kills).
- **Cost:** Permanent loss of swimming; seawater and sea-stone drain the eater.
- **Limits:** Power is fixed in type (body-alteration, element-becoming, beast-form); mastery and "awakening" extend it.
- **Taboo:** Eating a second fruit; extracting powers by killing eaters (the fruit reappears nearby after an eater dies).
- **Acquisition:** Item — found, traded, inherited, auctioned. Also a parallel training system (will-armour, foresight, overbearing will) for non-eaters (could link to Vowfire).

**Wielders:**
- **The Admiralty** — a global navy that hoards fruits and issues them to officers.
- **Pirate federations / sea lords** — balance of power held by a few fruit-eaters.
- **The Heaven-born Families** — untouchable nobility who "own" the seas.
- **Fruit auction houses & fences.**
- **Fruit-researchers** — scientists trying to synthesize artificial fruit.
- **Islanders** — inland peoples who never go to sea and view eaters as cursed.

**Subthemes:**
- **The Grand Course:** a dangerous sea-route where islands each have their own weather and logic.
- **Artificial fruit factory:** a warlord mass-producing flawed fruit that eat their eaters' smiles.
- **The erased century:** stone tablets in a forbidden script; archaeologists hunted to extinction.
- **Island of the beast-eaters:** a society of animal-form eaters forming a herd-kingdom.
- **Sea-stone mines:** slave labour mining the one material that negates fruit.
- **The fruit inheritance:** a family's fruit passes to whoever is nearest at death — succession by murder.
- **The fishfolk question:** sea-peoples oppressed by land powers; the fruit-curse doesn't touch them.
- **The legendary treasure:** a dead king's hint that triggered the age of piracy.
- **Awakening:** a few eaters reshape the world around them; the government calls them disasters.
- **The Stillwater band:** a band of windless sea crawling with deep-leviathans that sea-stone hulls can sneak through.

**Mysteries / dig sites:**
- Tablets of indestructible stone carved in a script it is illegal to read.
- An island whose trees bear swirl-marked fruit that rot instantly.
- A sunken palace whose throne is made of sea-stone.
- Fruit-shaped fossils older than any human settlement.
- A census of fruits: a book listing every known power, with blank pages that fill in themselves.

**Adds:**
- Deep-leviathans (vast sea monsters); fish-folk and merfolk.
- Fruit-eaters of three kinds (element-bodies, beast-hybrids, oddities).
- Sea-stone manacles, nets and hulls.
- Artificial fruit and its broken eaters.
- Bounty posters as world currency of fame.
- Island-needles (compasses that lock onto the next island).

**Progression:**
1. Fruit as myth: rare miracle-eaters worshipped or feared.
2. Cataloguing: encyclopaedia of fruits, the sea-stone discovery.
3. Navy era: centralized hoarding, bounty system.
4. Synthetic fruit research; lineage-factor science (cloning).
5. Awakening and the reveal of the erased history.

**Map effects:**
- Archipelago worlds, with a ring-continent and a belt of calm seas.
- Sea-stone fortresses at choke points.
- Islands with impossible climates (one is all summer, one is all snow) from fruit wars.

**Fragments:**
- **Navy encyclopedia of fruits** — catalogues powers, deliberately mislabels dangerous ones.
- **Pirate tavern song** — exaggerated, but the only source naming the old kingdom.
- **Forbidden tablet** — accurate, but readable only by a dying people.
- **Fishfolk oral history** — calls the fruits "stolen songs"; dismissed as myth.

**Tags:** slots `power-system-external`, `economy-resource`, `state-control`, `world-edge` (the sea) · tone heroic, whimsical (grim undercurrent) · era renaissance–industrial (age of sail) · scale personal→world.

## 5. The Bound Nine

**Pitch:** A handful of colossal beasts made from the world's own life-force are sealed inside human hosts — living weapons owned by nations, and hated by the villages they protect.

**Inspirations:** Naruto (chakra, tailed beasts, jinchūriki, the Sage of Six Paths, the divine tree and the alien progenitor), Avatar (spirit world), Pokémon (legendary beasts), Japanese kitsune/kaiju myth, Inuyasha (sealed demon).

**Core truth (choose one):**
1. **The beasts are one being.** The nine were split from a single world-tree monster by the first sage; reuniting them recreates it — which is the goal of a cult and, secretly, of the beasts themselves.
2. **The life-force was stolen.** All human energy-channels descend from an outsider who ate the fruit of a world-tree; the beasts are what is left of the tree's anger. The outsider's kin are coming back to harvest it.
3. **The hosts are the prison and the key.** Each sealing ages the land around its host; the nations' prosperity depends on hosts suffering. A host who befriends their beast ends the seal and the subsidy.

**Rules:**
- **Source:** Internal energy-channels (body + mind energy mixed); beasts are vast pure reservoirs.
- **Cost:** Exhaustion; channel damage; hosts endure the beast's hostility.
- **Limits:** Natural affinity to one element; hand-signs or gestures shape technique; beast power is only partly accessible without the beast's cooperation.
- **Taboo:** Extracting a beast (kills the host); forbidden scrolls (resurrection, body-swapping, soul-transfer); eye-transplants from bloodline clans.
- **Acquisition:** Trained (everyone has channels); bloodline traits (eyes, bones); sealing (forced on infants).

**Wielders:**
- **Hidden village-states** — each guarding one or two beasts as deterrent.
- **Shadow-ranked military leaders.**
- **Eye-clans** — inherit perception powers; feuding and massacred.
- **Rogue sealers** — collect beasts for a cult.
- **Sage-hermits** — learn nature energy from sacred animals (toads, snakes, slugs — our version: herons, boars, moths).

**Subthemes:**
- **The orphan host:** shunned child, hidden heritage, the village's secret.
- **Massacre of the eye-clan:** the survivor seeks vengeance and finds the state ordered it.
- **The beast-collecting cult:** cloaked mercenaries hunting hosts one by one.
- **Arms race of villages:** war over beast counts.
- **The tree returns:** a vast tree rising, draining all living energy.
- **Sage training:** learning world energy at risk of turning to stone.
- **The swamp of failed hosts:** sealing experiments that went wrong.
- **Beasts' rights:** a host who frees and befriends the beast; nations panic.
- **Moon-prison:** the progenitor sealed in the moon (our version: in a second moon / inside a mountain).
- **The proxy war:** small nations used as battlefields.

**Mysteries / dig sites:**
- A stone tablet readable only with the clan's eyes, revealing different text at each level of power.

- **Alchemist guild price list** — reveals scarcity long before anyone admits it.

## 23. The Underchord

**Pitch:** The world was sung into being and the song has never stopped; those who can hear it can join in — healing, building, unmaking — and somewhere a discordant voice has been singing against the theme since the beginning.

**Inspirations:** Tolkien's *Silmarillion* (the Ainulindalë and Melkor's discord), C.S. Lewis's *The Magician's Nephew* (a world sung into being), the *Kalevala* (Väinämöinen's songs of origin), Aboriginal Australian songlines (as a distant reference only — not to be reproduced), Anne McCaffrey's *Crystal Singer* and Harper Hall, Symphogear, Ocarina of Time, the Hindu concept *Nāda Brahma* ("the world is sound").

**Core truth (choose one):**
1. **The discord belongs in the song.** The great enemy's counter-theme was meant to be woven in; the choirs have spent ages drowning it out, and by doing so made the world brittle and the enemy monstrous. True harmony requires letting it in.
2. **The song is ending.** The original singers have fallen silent one by one; each lost voice was an extinct species, a vanished element, a forgotten colour. When the last voice stops, the world goes quiet.
3. **We are notes, not singers.** Mortal songsmiths aren't singing — they're being sung; every life is a phrase in a longer composition, and one mortal has learned to read ahead in the score and rewrite it.

**Rules:**
- **Source:** The world's underlying music; each thing has its own note and rhythm.
- **Cost:** The voice (literally — overuse destroys throat and hearing); sanity (hearing the song constantly); a song once sung cannot be unsung.
- **Limits:** Must match the target's note; choirs amplify enormously; solo singers are subtle, not strong; distance limited by hearing.
- **Taboo:** Singing the counter-theme; *silencing* (unmaking a thing's note entirely); stealing a voice; singing a person's death-song before their time.
- **Acquisition:** Born with a perfect ear; trained in conservatories; gifted by a singer-spirit; deafness sometimes paradoxically opens deeper hearing.

**Wielders:**
- **The Choirs** — temple- and state-orchestras that maintain city walls, harvests and weather.
- **Wandering bards** — news-carriers, spies, unlicensed singers.
- **Crystal-cutters** — mine resonant crystal by singing it free.
- **The Silent Order** — monks who never speak; the only ones who can safely unmake.
- **Discord cults** — singers of the counter-theme.
- **Drum-clans** — rhythm-singers of the steppe whose magic is martial.

**Subthemes:**
- **The song-paths:** routes that can only be walked by singing them; maps that are melodies.
- **The silent city:** a capital where no sound carries; something ate its note.
- **The discordant heir:** a royal child who can only sing the counter-theme.
- **Bardic network:** news and propaganda spread in ballads; censorship of songs.
- **Crystal mines:** singers go deaf and mad cutting resonance-crystal for the rich.
- **Choir wars:** rival modes, rival churches; a heresy of a single wrong interval.
- **The lost verse:** a missing stanza of the creation song is hidden in a children's rhyme.
- **Lullaby wards:** mothers' songs as the oldest protective magic.
- **Dirge-craft:** singing the dead to rest — or back.
- **Instruments of power:** a horn that topples walls, a drum that calls storms, a harp strung with a dragon's sinew.

**Mysteries / dig sites:**
- A cave that has hummed one note for ten thousand years.
- An amphitheatre whose acoustics heal anyone who sleeps on the stage.
- A bell buried under a lake that tolls when the realm is in danger.
- A valley where birds don't sing and echoes don't return.
- Fossil "sound-stones" that replay ancient voices when struck.
- A broken organ in a ruined cathedral with pipes reaching underground.

**Adds:**
- Songbirds that carry spells; echo-creatures; silence-wraiths (beings of no-note).
- Bards, choirmasters, crystal-cutters, silent monks.
- Instruments of power; resonant crystals; tuning-forks used as wards.
- Discordant spawn (creatures that are "wrong notes" — painful to look at).

**Progression:**
1. Chants, drums, lullabies.
2. Temple choirs, modes and scales as sacred law.
3. Notation: written music as spell-scrolls (anyone can perform).
4. Mechanical instruments: music boxes and organs as automatic magic.
5. Recording: captured song-power; industrial resonance engines.

**Map effects:**
- Song-paths crossing continents (visible as worn, straight tracks).
- Resonance cities ringed with bell-towers.
- Silent zones (grey, birdless) on the map.
- Crystal mountains that ring in the wind.

**Fragments:**
- **The official hymnal** — the creation song with the counter-theme verses removed.
- **A bard's ballad** — politically slanted; often the only record of an event.
- **A silent monk's tablet** — written, never spoken; dry and precise.
- **A sound-stone** — fragmentary primary source of a forgotten voice.
- **A discord cult score** — beautiful and terrifying; claims the evil was a victim.

**Tags:** slots `cosmology`, `magic-source`, `power-system-personal`, `divine-order` · tone mythic, whimsical, melancholic · era stone–renaissance · scale personal→world.

## Appendix B — Generator notes

- **One truth per world.** Each premise lists 1–3 core-truth options; roll one and suppress fragments that contradict it (or keep them as deliberate misinformation from biased sources).
- **Fragments are the reveal engine.** Every fragment type has a stated bias. The generator should distribute at least one "official/biased", one "outsider/heretic" and one "primary/raw" fragment per premise so players can triangulate.
- **Subthemes map to regions.** Assign 1–2 subthemes per region based on biome and polity (e.g. desert → Long Sight water economy; archipelago → Hungerfruit, First Tongue; mountains → Ascending Path sects).
- **Progression ↔ tech level.** Use the progression ladder index as a soft requirement: a medieval world should not reach progression stage 5 of Glyphwright Science unless a premise explicitly says the magic is ahead of the tech.
- **Naming hygiene.** Inspirations lines are developer-only metadata and must never be emitted into world text. All other vocabulary in this file is intended to be original or generic; a final pass in code should block a list of source-work proper nouns.
