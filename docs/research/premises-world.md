# World Premises — World-Structure, Cosmology, Mystery, Mythic & Anomaly

Research catalogue for Worlds Infinite's **World Premises** system: the 1–2 hidden backbone ideas that make a generated world feel special. This file covers premises about *the world itself*: its shape, its edges, its history, its gods and its secrets. Personal power systems and sci-fi technology are out of scope (see sibling catalogues).

**Rules used here**

- Each premise is written "in the essence of" well-known fiction and myth, then reworked under an **original name**. The *Inspirations* line is for our reference only and must never leak into generated output. Generated text should use only the premise's own vocabulary (our names for places, creatures, factions).
- Similar sources were merged (e.g. falling moon + time loops + stopped time are one premise; fae otherworld + mirror/shadow world are one premise with variants; world-on-a-beast + dead-god's-body are one premise). The result is **28 premises**.
- Every premise offers several **Core truth** options so two worlds using the same premise can still hide a different secret.

**Tag vocabulary**

- **Slots** (a world should normally fill each slot at most once; two premises claiming the same *exclusive* slot conflict unless the entry says they combine):
  - `world-shape` (exclusive): the physical form of the world (disc, sphere, hollow, islands in void, body of a being).
  - `world-edge` (exclusive): what lies at or beyond the boundary of the known map.
  - `cosmology`: what reality is made of / what lies outside it.
  - `divine-order`: who rules, made or holds up the world.
  - `apocalypse`: the threatened or ongoing end.
  - `afterlife`: what happens to the dead.
  - `time`: how time behaves (cycles, loops, stoppage).
  - `history-secret`: the buried truth of the past.
  - `ecology`: a dominant, premise-driven ecosystem.
- **Tone**: mythic, grim, horror, wonder, melancholy (one or two primary).
- **Tech era range**: stone, bronze, iron, medieval, renaissance, early-industrial, industrial (fantasy-flavoured). Given as min–max where the premise works best.
- **Scale**: local (a region/site), continental, world, cosmic.

**Map hooks** are flagged with **[MAP]** inside "How it shapes the world" because the map generator needs them most.

## 1. The Nursery Sea

*Everything you have ever mapped is a single sheltered basin; the true world wraps around it, vast, ancient and lethal.*

**Inspirations:** Hunter x Hunter (the known world as an island in a lake surrounded by the Dark Continent; returning expeditions bring back calamities; survival rates near zero); medieval "here be dragons" mapping; Lovecraft's *At the Mountains of Madness*; *The Truman Show*; the Garden of Eden/Hesperides as a fenced paradise; the Norse Midgard fence against Utgard.

**Core truth (pick 1–3):**
1. **The Nursery:** the basin is a deliberately sheltered enclosure built by an older power to raise young peoples until they are "ready"; the outer world is the real inheritance and the real danger.
2. **The Quarantine:** the basin is a refuge carved out after the outer world fell to something (a calamity, a living pathogen, a war of gods). The outer world isn't merely dangerous — it's *occupied*.
3. **The Reversal:** the outer world is fine. *We* are the quarantined thing: the basin was sealed to keep the inner peoples (or something they carry) from escaping.

**How it shapes the world**
- **[MAP]** The playable map is a large inland sea/lake ringed by an encircling barrier: impassable storm belt, shoals, sheer cliffs, a misty "rim-ocean", or a ring of mountains. Beyond it: a dark, fogged, unrendered mega-landmass shown only as fragments on old charts. A few known "passes" (a strait, a dead current, a mountain notch) are guarded or forbidden.
- **Climate:** the basin is suspiciously temperate and stable; storms intensify toward the rim. Weather from outside occasionally "spills in" carrying alien seeds, spores, ash.
- **History:** an official record of a handful of past expeditions (each a disaster), suppressed by treaty. Civilisations treat the rim as both sacred frontier and taboo.
- **Peoples:** basin nations who have never seen the outer world; rim-dwellers (lighthouse clans, wreckers) who see glimpses; a tiny population of returnees and their descendants, sometimes changed.

**Factions & ideologies**
- *The Compact of the Rim* — an alliance of basin powers that forbids unsanctioned voyages (fear + monopoly).
- *The Far-Seekers* — explorer guild/creed that believes the outer world is humanity's birthright.
- *Gatekeeper tribes* — rim peoples paid (or bound by ancient oath) to turn back ships.
- *The Returned* — a secretive fellowship of survivors and their kin; some carry outer "gifts".
- Ideologies: isolationism vs manifest destiny; "the shelter is a mercy" vs "the shelter is a cage".

**Subthemes / arcs**
1. A sanctioned expedition is being assembled; every power wants seats aboard.
2. A calamity brought back long ago is still contained in a vault under a capital.
3. A rim nation grows rich from salvaged outer-world goods and lies about where they come from.
4. A child is born to a returnee lineage with impossible traits.
5. Charts from a past voyage are forged, stolen and fought over.
6. A sickness or creature from outside slips through a storm-gap and spreads.
7. A lighthouse clan's ancestral duty is revealed to be keeping something *in*, not out.
8. A guide-race from beyond appears at a rim port, asking for something.
9. Two powers race to claim the only safe pass.
10. A heretic cartographer proves the basin is far smaller than claimed.
11. Ancient boundary stones are failing, and the rim storms are weakening.

**Mysteries / dig sites / anomalies**
- The **Seventh Hull**: a wreck of the last failed expedition washed up with no crew and fresh food aboard.
- **Boundary stelae** carved in an unknown script, all facing inward.
- A rim island where compasses point *outward* to a single place.
- A vault of sealed jars labelled by expedition number.
- Sea-caves where tides bring in plants that do not exist in the basin.
- A mountain pass where travellers hear voices calling their names from beyond.

**Creatures / people / items it adds**
- Calamity-beasts (one-of-a-kind outer threats: a wish-granting creature with a terrible cost, a disease of immortality, a plant that eats ships).
- Rim-guides — hereditary pilots who know safe currents.
- Outer relics: objects of enormous power, each catalogued with a death toll.
- "Returnee" lineage traits (eyes that see in the fog, inability to age normally).

**Knowledge progression**
1. The sea's rim is the end of the world; legends speak of "the dark beyond".
2. Some ships came back; they found land. Their reports are sealed.
3. The rim barrier is artificial or maintained (stones, rituals, a living storm).
4. The outer world is enormous; the basin is a small fraction of it.
5. Why the basin exists (Nursery / Quarantine / Reversal) — and who built it.
6. Contact with the builders or their successors; the terms of "readiness".

**Fragments**
- *Compact expedition logs* — accurate but heavily redacted; survival numbers falsified downward.
- *Returnee testimony* — vivid, partly hallucinated, often coerced.
- *Rim-tribe oath songs* — preserve the true purpose of the barrier in ritual language they no longer understand.
- *Merchant charts* — tempting but deliberately misleading to protect trade routes.

**Tags:** slots `world-edge`, `history-secret` · tone: wonder, horror · era: iron–early-industrial · scale: world. Combines well with Elder Lattice (builders), Walking Mountains (outer fauna), Ward-Walls (land variant). Conflicts with The Rim (both are world-edge).

## 2. The Ward-Walls

*Humanity survives behind colossal walls against monsters — and the history explaining the walls is a lie.*

**Inspirations:** Attack on Titan (three concentric walls, enemies whose origin is human, a king who erased memory, a sea and a whole world outside); Hadrian's Wall and the Great Wall; *The Maze Runner*; *The Village* (M. Night Shyamalan); *The City of Ember*; ASOIAF's Wall.

**Core truth (pick 1–3):**
1. **The monsters are us:** the besiegers are transformed people (exiles, a conquered nation, the walled people's own ancestors), and the transformation can be undone or weaponised.
2. **The walls are the weapon:** the walls themselves are dormant giants, a sleeping army, or a spell that will one day turn outward.
3. **The world moved on:** outside the walls, other nations thrive and consider the walled people either a lost relic or a feared weapon-race; the "monsters" are a buffer kept in place by a pact.

**How it shapes the world**
- **[MAP]** Concentric or chained wall rings around a heartland; districts that bulge out from the walls as fortified gates; abandoned lands between rings; beyond the outermost wall, a no-man's-land with ruined towns; very far beyond, a coastline or another country never shown on internal maps.
- **Climate:** normal; the outside is often lusher (untouched by agriculture).
- **History:** an official "Founding" story starting about a century ago; anything before is missing. Records were burned; memories were taken.
- **Peoples:** a crowded walled population stratified by ring (inner = privileged); a hidden royal line; outsiders who live free beyond and know far more.

**Factions & ideologies**
- *The Garrison* — maintains walls; defensive, conservative.
- *The Outward Corps* — scouts who venture beyond; high death rate, despised and heroic.
- *The Wall Faith* — worships the walls as divine; guards a secret inside them.
- *The Throne-in-Shadow* — the true royal line that maintains the lie.
- *Outer nations* — who treat the walled people as a weapon or a sin.
- Ideologies: safety in ignorance vs freedom with truth; inherited guilt.

**Subthemes / arcs**
1. A gate breach floods an outer ring with monsters.
2. A scout finds a book in the wilds written in the walled people's language, dated before the Founding.
3. A recruit discovers they can become one of the monsters.
4. The Wall Faith forbids repair of a crack — because of what is visible through it.
5. Coup in the inner ring over the true heir.
6. A stranger arrives from beyond claiming to be an ambassador.
7. Refugees from a fallen ring overwhelm the next ring's food supply.
8. Excavation under the inner capital reveals the "Founding" cavern.
9. A monster seems to recognise a person and does not attack.
10. The outside world sends a warship.

**Mysteries / dig sites**
- A face visible in a wall crack, eye open.
- A cellar in a frontier town containing the true history and three photographs/paintings of a city by the sea.
- The "Founding Cavern" with crystal-encased remains.
- A forest of giant trees where monsters never enter.
- Monster corpses that evaporate, leaving only a human-sized hollow.
- An abandoned outer village where everyone left in one night, doors open.

**Adds**
- Wall-besiegers (mindless giant humanoids or chimeras) with a human core.
- Wall-crawlers and wall-hooks (gear for climbing/fighting giants).
- The Founder-line: a bloodline that can command the besiegers.
- Memory-sealing relic (crown, song, serum).

**Knowledge progression**
1. The walls protect us; nothing exists outside but monsters.
2. Monsters behave strangely and are not purely animal.
3. Monsters were people; the Founding story is falsified.
4. There is a world beyond with nations, and they know about us.
5. The walls/royal line hold a weapon or pact; the truth of the original war.

**Fragments**
- *Wall Faith scripture* — mythologised but contains literal truths about the walls.
- *Royal archive* — complete but falsified at the root.
- *Scout journals* — honest, fragmentary, often the authors died mid-entry.
- *Outsider texts* — accurate about the outside, propagandistic about the walled people.

**Tags:** slots `history-secret`, `world-edge` (local variant) · tone: grim, horror · era: medieval–early-industrial · scale: continental. Combines with Nursery Sea (walls inside a basin), Unremembering, Blood Debt. Conflicts with Strewn Sky.

## 3. The Downward Throat

*At the centre of the world a bottomless pit descends through impossible layers of wonder. Going down is easy; coming back up costs you.*

**Inspirations:** Made in Abyss (layered abyss; curse that strikes on ascent, worsening per layer from nausea to loss of humanity to death; relics; whistle ranks for delvers); Dante's *Inferno* circles; *Journey to the Center of the Earth*; Moria/Khazad-dûm; Diablo's descending levels; dungeon-crawl tradition; the Greek Tartarus.

**Core truth (pick 1–3):**
1. **The Field:** the pit generates a force-field that binds what descends; the "curse" is the field resisting the theft of anything (including your soul) from the depths.
2. **The Wound:** the pit is a wound in the world-body, and the curse is the world's immune response.
3. **The Invitation:** the pit is an intended path — something at the bottom is calling, and the curse is a filter that lets only the transformed reach it.

**How it shapes the world**
- **[MAP]** A vast round chasm in the middle of an island, plain or city. The town grows on its rim in tiers. The surrounding region is rich and crowded; the rest of the world is a hinterland of relic-markets. Each layer below has its own biome (forest of inverted trees, a sea of goblets, a ring of waterfalls, eternal mist) — useful for local maps.
- **Climate:** the pit breathes; updrafts create a local weather system, a permanent cloud column visible across the continent.
- **History:** the pit was discovered (or "opened") a few thousand years ago; a lost civilisation's ruins line the walls; every era sent deeper expeditions.
- **Peoples:** delver clans; relic merchants; orphan apprentices; deep-dwellers who chose never to return and changed.

**Factions & ideologies**
- *The Delvers' Guild* — ranks delvers by colour/token; the highest are legends who never come up.
- *The Rim Authority* — taxes relics, runs the orphanage that supplies cheap delvers.
- *Relic cartels* in distant capitals that fund expeditions.
- *The Descenders* — a creed that wants to reach the bottom regardless of cost.
- *Experimenters* — scholars who test the curse on people.

**Subthemes / arcs**
1. A legendary delver's token surfaces with a message: "come down".
2. An orphan apprentice discovers they are immune to the curse.
3. A layer is "moving upward" — its biome appears at shallower depth.
4. A relic of world-changing power is found and every crown wants it.
5. Experiments on children in a deep camp.
6. Deep-dwellers trade with surface towns via a basket rope.
7. The cloud column changes colour.
8. A rival pit opens on another continent.
9. A layer's creatures begin to climb.
10. A cult wants to drop a whole city into the pit.

**Mysteries / dig sites**
- The **Fourth Ledge** temple with statues facing downward.
- Ruins of a city built *upside-down* on the shaft wall.
- A rope ladder of unknown fibre hanging from the darkness above the bottom layer.
- Relics that only function at certain depths.
- A village of transformed humans who remember being surface people.
- A wind that blows *down* once a year.

**Adds**
- Delvers with rank tokens.
- Layer-fauna: apex predators that hunt by sound, mimics, floating jellies.
- Relics graded by tier (curiosities → world-breakers).
- The "Hollowed" — those transformed by the deep curse.

**Knowledge progression**
1. The pit is a source of relics; ascent sickness is a known hazard.
2. Layers are mapped; curse severity correlates with depth.
3. The curse is a field with rules (it's triggered by ascent, can be shielded).
4. A previous civilisation descended entirely.
5. What is at the bottom, and why it calls.

**Fragments**
- *Guild depth charts* — accurate to known depth; deeper entries are hearsay.
- *Legend-delver letters* sent up by mechanism — truthful but cryptic.
- *Relic merchants' catalogues* — inflated provenance.
- *Murals in the shaft ruins* — ancient view, suggests the pit was *made*.

**Tags:** slots `world-shape` (local feature, non-exclusive), `cosmology` · tone: wonder, horror · era: medieval–industrial · scale: local–continental. Combines with Elder Lattice, Inner Sun (pit leads to the inner world), Body of the World (pit as wound).

## 4. The Last Kindling

*Every good thing in the world is warmed by a primordial flame that is guttering out. It can be kept alive by burning great souls — or allowed to die.*

**Inspirations:** Dark Souls (the First Flame; lords linking the fire; the Age of Fire vs the Age of Dark; undead curse; cycle repeated by each Chosen); Zoroastrian sacred fire; the Vestal flame of Rome; Prometheus; the hearth-fire as civilisation; entropy/heat-death.

**Core truth (pick 1–3):**
1. **The Unnatural Fire:** the fire is not natural order. The gods of fire are clinging to their age; the Dark that follows is the age of mortals.
2. **The Real Necessity:** the Dark truly is annihilation; but feeding the fire degrades every age — each rekindling is smaller and colder.
3. **Both Lies:** the fire and the dark are two halves of one organism; the cycle can be broken only by a third choice (usurping, merging, or leaving the world).

**How it shapes the world**
- **[MAP]** A central kiln/crater or citadel where the flame is tended; a radial pattern of "bonfire roads" (pilgrimage routes with shrines); outer zones of decay where the flame's warmth no longer reaches — grey, rotting, haunted lands. Older ages' kingdoms are *stacked* on top of one another (ruins inside ruins).
- **Climate:** cooling over the last centuries; the sun weakening; days shortening; ash snow.
- **History:** a chain of named ages, each ended by a "linking"; each age's capital rebuilt on the previous one.
- **Peoples:** dwindling lords; mortals who become hollow/undying when the fire fails; pilgrims; dark-dwellers who welcome the night.

**Factions & ideologies**
- *The Keepers of the Hearth* — priesthood that seeks the next soul to burn.
- *The Embers* — the chosen/cursed undying who are candidates.
- *The Night Patient* — heretics/serpents who preach the coming dark as liberation.
- *Usurpers* — those who want to seize the flame themselves.
- Ideologies: duty and sacrifice vs letting go; fear of change vs fear of decline.

**Subthemes / arcs**
1. The undying curse spreads; families hide their marked.
2. A dead lord's throne is empty; pilgrims race to claim his soul.
3. A kingdom from a prior age pokes through a collapsed valley.
4. The Keepers kidnap a hero for sacrifice.
5. A dark-cult offers mortals an eternal night of freedom.
6. The sun visibly dims; crops fail in the north.
7. A painted world inside a painting hides refugees from the cycle.
8. Last fire-dragon/elder-beast seeks revenge on the fire-gods.
9. A city of hollowed people still running its markets.
10. Someone finds evidence of the age *before* the fire.

**Mysteries / dig sites**
- The **Kiln** itself, ash-choked, surrounded by armour of earlier sacrifices.
- Stacked cities in a single canyon.
- A bonfire that burns black.
- A grave of the "first lord" with a fake body.
- Gold-and-ash murals depicting a world of grey stone trees.
- A ring of shrines that no longer light.

**Adds**
- The Undying (marked mortals who return after death, slowly losing self).
- Soul-currency.
- Fire-keepers (bound maidens or monks).
- Primordial beasts of the pre-fire era (stone dragons, serpents).

**Knowledge progression**
1. The fire is holy and eternal.
2. The fire fades; gods have fed it before.
3. Each age is weaker; the curse is a symptom.
4. Who started the cycle, and what the dark actually is.
5. The third option exists.

**Fragments**
- *Keeper liturgy* — pro-fire, omits costs.
- *Serpent whispers* — partial truth, self-interested.
- *Item descriptions/inscriptions on relics* — terse, accurate, scattered.
- *Painted chronicles from older ages* — contradict current history.

**Tags:** slots `apocalypse`, `cosmology`, `time` (cycle) · tone: melancholy, grim · era: bronze–medieval · scale: world. Combines with Unquiet Gate, Long Dark, Wheel of Suns (merge cycles). Conflicts with Pale Visitor (both claim the sky-source of light) unless combined deliberately.

## 5. The Broken Writ

*The world was governed by a single sacred law made manifest. It was shattered; the demigods who took the shards are tearing the world apart.*

**Inspirations:** Elden Ring (the Elden Ring shattered; demigods hold Great Runes; the Shattering war; an outer god's will; the Erdtree's grace); the Tablet of Destinies in Mesopotamian myth (stolen by Anzû); Sauron's ring; the Holy Grail; Tolkien's Silmarils; the Ark of the Covenant.

**Core truth (pick 1–3):**
1. **Imposed Law:** the Writ was imposed by an outer being to suppress the world's native order (death, change, chaos), and the shattering was a rebellion.
2. **Shattered on Purpose:** the queen/god who ruled shattered it herself — to escape the law she was bound by.
3. **Many Writs:** the Writ was always one of many possible orders; whoever reassembles it chooses the next one.

**How it shapes the world**
- **[MAP]** Several demigod domains, each with a distinctive biome distorted by its shard (a rotting swamp, a frozen observatory, a lava-tower, an underground star-river); a central capital beneath a radiant, now-sickly tree or monolith; walls of thorns sealing the capital; battle-scarred lands between domains.
- **Climate:** each domain has an anomalous climate; the heartland is golden-hazed and timeless.
- **History:** a long golden age under the Writ, then the Shattering war; now a stalemate of broken demigods.
- **Peoples:** exiles who lost the Writ's grace; demigod courts; old natives repressed by the Writ (giants, star-folk, death-touched).

**Factions & ideologies**
- *The Shard-Courts* — each demigod and their following.
- *The Graceless* — exiles called back to claim the shards.
- *The Old Order loyalists* — want the Writ restored as-was.
- *Pre-Writ peoples* — seek to reinstate their own order.
- *Outer-god cults* — each pushing a different alien law (frenzy, rot, stars).

**Subthemes / arcs**
1. A demigod's domain is rotting from within.
2. A forgotten death-order resurfaces; corpses walk.
3. A star-sorcerer wants to steal fate from the sky.
4. Giants' furnace on a mountain is the true seal of something.
5. Exiles converge on the capital.
6. A child demigod is being grafted with stolen limbs.
7. A tree's roots draw dead into its catacombs.
8. A rune-shard falls into a peasant's hands.
9. The capital's tree has been burned before.

**Mysteries / dig sites**
- The broken monolith in the capital.
- A buried eternal city under the earth with a false sky.
- Catacombs full of the uncremated.
- A tower whose top floor has no door.
- Statues of a god whose name is chiselled out.

**Adds**
- Shards/Runes (give dominion over one domain of reality).
- Demigods (powerful, broken heirs).
- Grace-lights (beacons that guide the chosen).
- Grafted horrors; star-born beasts.

**Knowledge progression**
1. The Writ shattered; the war continues.
2. The shards can be reassembled.
3. The Writ was imposed and suppressed something.
4. The shattering was intentional.
5. Choose the next order.

**Fragments**
- *Court histories* — each glorifying their demigod.
- *Old Order texts* — idealised golden age.
- *Native peoples' memories* — pre-Writ truths, persecuted.
- *Inscriptions on shards* — literal law-text.

**Tags:** slots `divine-order`, `history-secret` · tone: mythic, grim · era: bronze–medieval · scale: continental–world. Combines with Great Root, Gods' Twilight, Splinter World. Conflicts with Last Kindling only if both claim the central sacred object.

## 6. The Unlidded Eye

*Beyond the sky sleep gods older than the world. To learn about them is to change — and the more you see, the less human you are.*

**Inspirations:** H.P. Lovecraft (Cthulhu sleeping in R'lyeh; Azathoth; the Mythos's indifference); Bloodborne (insight; Great Ones; blood ministration; the hunt and the nightmare); *Annihilation*; *Call of Cthulhu* RPG sanity; Warhammer's warp gods; Kafka; Junji Ito.

**Core truth (pick 1–3):**
1. **Indifferent Sleepers:** alien gods sleep beneath the sea/sky; dreams leak; human knowledge awakens receptivity in the brain. They are not evil — they don't notice us.
2. **Seeded Ascension:** the gods intended to make heirs; humans who "see" are candidates to become new gods (and monsters along the way).
3. **The Waking:** a star alignment approaches; when the eye opens, the world ends — or transforms.

**How it shapes the world**
- **[MAP]** Coastal cities with drowned temples; a sunken alien city that rises at certain tides; observatories on mountain peaks; a quarantined city where "the hunt" occurs; isolated fishing villages; ruins beneath a scholarly town.
- **Climate:** fogs, unnatural tides, auroras; a blood-moon.
- **History:** an old cult found the sleeper's remains, built a healing church, used its blood — leading to transformations.
- **Peoples:** scholars; healing church; fishermen with "the look"; hunters.

**Factions & ideologies**
- *The Ministry of Blood* — a church that uses alien relics for healing; hides the truth.
- *The Scholarium* — academic society seeking direct communion.
- *The Hunters' Lodge* — exterminates beasts born of the transformation.
- *Deep-cults* in fishing towns.
- *Watchers* — secret society who erases knowledge.

**Subthemes / arcs**
1. A plague of beastliness after a church "blessing".
2. A scholar returns from an expedition with a skull of a god-child.
3. A fishing village with no young people.
4. A night of the hunt — streets sealed until dawn.
5. A stargazer sees a new star, which is an eye.
6. A noble family grows tall and silent.
7. A dream-realm with a shared population.
8. A ship returns from a reef that is not on charts.
9. A child prophet draws the same shape.

**Mysteries / dig sites**
- A fossil of a giant with too many eyes.
- A labyrinth beneath the city with older walls the lower you go.
- The temple with angles that don't add up.
- A fresh "god-corpse" washed up on a beach.
- A clock tower that marks the time until awakening.

**Adds**
- Seeing (a tracked knowledge stat that unlocks perception and invites madness).
- Beast-men; star-children; eye-flowers.
- Alien relics (bloods, eggs, cords).
- Hunters with transforming weapons.

**Knowledge progression**
1. Strange cults and sicknesses.
2. The church's healing is alien in origin.
3. The sleepers are real and dreaming.
4. Humans can ascend.
5. The awakening date.

**Fragments**
- *Ministry gospel* — sanitised.
- *Scholarium journals* — accurate but the authors went mad.
- *Fisher folk songs* — accurate in metaphor.
- *Dream records* — real but uninterpretable.

**Tags:** slots `cosmology`, `divine-order`, `apocalypse` (optional) · tone: horror, grim · era: renaissance–industrial · scale: cosmic. Combines with Drowned Crown, Dreamer Beneath, Leavings. Conflicts with Pale Visitor (both claim a cosmic visitor) — combine only as rivals.

## 7. The Dreamer Beneath

*The world is the dream of a sleeping god — or is being poisoned by one. When dreams spread like plague, the dreamer stirs.*

## Appendix B: Generator notes

- **Pairing rule of thumb:** pick one *structural* premise (fills `world-shape` or `world-edge`: 1, 3, 11, 12, 13, 21, 22, 28) plus one *secret* premise (fills `history-secret`/`divine-order`/`apocalypse`: 2, 4–10, 14–20, 23–27). Structural premises drive the map; secret premises drive lore, factions and dig sites.
- **Strong pairings (examples):** Nursery Sea + Elder Lattice (the builders of the basin); Strewn Sky + Drowned Crown (isles raised above a drowned world); Body of the World + Gods' Twilight (the murderers rule, the giant's kin bring the end); Rim + Body of the World (Bearer variant carries the disc); Downward Throat + Inner Sun (the pit leads inside); Long Dark + Ward-Walls (the ice wall); Unquiet Gate + Last Kindling (fading fire means the dead stop leaving); Splinter World + Broken Writ (law and land shattered together); Falling Hour + Unremembering (only loopers remember).
- **Merges, not pairs:** Unlidded Eye + Dreamer Beneath (one sleeping god, not two); Wheel of Suns + Last Kindling (one cycle); Thinning (variant 3) + Unremembering (the lost world eats memories).
- **Knowledge progression** steps map naturally onto in-game eras: step 1 at founding, the last step only reachable late or through player action. Fragments should be generated as in-world documents with a reliability score and a bias tag (e.g. `pro-church`, `redacted`, `hallucinated`, `propaganda`, `true-but-cryptic`).
- **Name hygiene:** only the premise's own vocabulary (place, creature and faction names written here, or procedurally generated ones in the same spirit) should appear in output. The Inspirations lines are reference-only.
