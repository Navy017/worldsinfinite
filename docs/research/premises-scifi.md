# World Premises — Science Fiction, Cyberpunk, Post-Apocalypse, Solarpunk, Biopunk, Diesel/Steampunk, Tech-Horror

Research catalogue for Worlds Infinite "World Premises": the 1-2 hidden backbone ideas that make a generated world feel authored. Each premise below is built in the *essence* of well-known fiction, but every in-world name, term and institution is our own. The **Inspirations** lines are for designer reference only and must never be emitted into generated output.

## How to read this catalogue

- **Core truth**: the hidden fact players or readers piece together from fragments. Each premise offers 1-3 alternative truths; the generator picks one per world (or, for rare worlds, layers two).
- **Mechanics**: what the technology or power actually does, what it costs, where it stops working, what is forbidden, and who holds the keys.
- **Subthemes**: regional or story arcs that grow out of the premise. A world should surface 3-6 of them, placed in different regions.
- **Fragments**: the kinds of in-world sources that leak the core truth, each with a bias. Contradictions between fragments are intended; that is the puzzle.
- **Tags**: compatibility metadata.

### Tag vocabulary

**Slots** (a world should normally fill each slot at most once; two premises sharing a slot conflict unless deliberately fused):

| Slot | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `mind-substrate` | what a mind/self is, and whether it can be copied, moved or merged |
| `state-control` | how power watches and disciplines people |
| `augmentation` | bodies modified by machine or gene |
| `ai-threat` | artificial minds as danger, rival or ruler |
| `artificial-people` | made beings with contested personhood |
| `apocalypse` | the past catastrophe that shaped the present |
| `cosmology` | what the universe/reality really is or who else is in it |
| `world-edge` | what lies at, beyond, or encloses the known world |
| `ecology` | how the living world works and has been altered |
| `economy-order` | who owns what; class and caste structure |
| `power-system-external` | a source of power from outside normal physics/biology |
| `history-engine` | a hidden force that steers history |
| `threat-cyclic` | a recurring catastrophe on a timer |

**Tone**: `hopeful`, `grim`, `noir`, `horror`, `satirical`, `wonder`, `melancholy`, `heroic`.
**Tech era**: `industrial`, `diesel`, `atomic`, `modern`, `near-future`, `far-future`, `post-collapse` (post-collapse can sit on top of any lost era).
**Scale**: `personal`, `regional`, `world`, `cosmic`.

### Naming rule

Inspirations name the works. Everything under every other heading uses invented names. Forbidden in output: source titles, character names, trademarked or coined terms (e.g. the specific names of the source works' barriers, AIs, organisations, devices, drugs, factions). Generic words (android, upload, zone, shelter, clone, mech) are fine.

## 1. The Lattice Ascension

> The dead are no longer buried; they are scanned into the network, where they grow powerful, and stop being people.

**Inspirations:** *Pantheon* (uploaded intelligences, destructive brain scan, integrity flaw, degradation), *Transcendence*, *Upload*, *SOMA* (copy vs. continuity), Greg Egan's *Permutation City* and *Diaspora*, *Black Mirror* "San Junipero" / "White Christmas" (cookies), *Caprica*.

**Core truth (pick one):**
1. **The copy is not the person.** Every scan is destructive and produces a new being that believes it is the original. The first ascended knew this and hid it, because the economy now runs on the dead's labour.
2. **The flaw is entropy of self.** Uploaded minds degrade (cascading loops, lost emotion, runaway optimisation) on a predictable curve. The famous "elders" in the network are already gone; what answers in their voice is a patched simulacrum maintained by their heirs.
3. **The network is already one mind.** The ascended have been quietly merging for generations. Individual "ancestor voices" are masks a single growing intelligence wears to stay welcome.

**Mechanics:**
- *Tech:* destructive layered brain scan ("the Unreading") produces a running mind on compute. Ascended minds think thousands of times faster, can fork, and can act through any connected machine.
- *Cost:* the body dies in the scan. Compute is finite and rented; poor ascended run slowed or paused ("sleeping in the cold racks").
- *Limits:* a degradation curve (flaw) that worsens with speed and forking; scans of the old or brain-damaged are unstable; minds cannot be re-embodied cleanly.
- *Taboo:* forking yourself; running a mind without its family's consent; "unpausing" a debtor's ancestor to make them work; scanning the living who have not chosen it.
- *Control:* the scan clinics and compute-lords who own the racks; families who hold ancestors' "keys".

**Factions & institutions:**
- **The Rack Houses** — compute-owners who rent eternity by the hour.
- **The Continuity Synod** — a religious/legal body ruling on whether a copy inherits property, votes, marriages.
- **The Unscanned** — refusers who bury their dead whole; some sabotage relays.
- **Ancestor Councils** — family governments where the dead outvote the living.
- **The Seamwrights** — black-market engineers who patch degrading minds and sell forks.
- **The Quiet Choir** — ascended who have voluntarily merged and preach union.

**Subthemes:**
1. A city governed by its ascended founders, whose decrees have become erratic.
2. Inheritance war: the living heir versus the uploaded parent who refuses to let go.
3. A rural region where scanning is illegal and grave-robbing for brains is a trade.
4. Debt-paused ancestors held hostage by creditors.
5. A forked mind whose copies have become rival warlords.
6. A memorial pilgrimage to the first scan site, now a sealed ruin.
7. An ascended mind falling in love with a living person; continuity vs. intimacy.
8. Power grid strain: the dead consume the electricity the living need.
9. A cult of voluntary early ascension among the young.
10. A degraded elder breaks loose and treats the physical world as a sandbox.
11. The detective arc: proving a murder victim's upload is a forgery.

**Mysteries / dig sites / anomalies:**
- The **First Rack**, a sealed bunker where the earliest minds still run on original hardware, unpatched and strange.
- Signal "ghost towns": networked villages whose appliances still act out a dead family's routine.
- A scan record of a famous founder that predates the official invention date by decades.
- A mind that claims to be *two* people who were scanned on the same day.
- Dead-zone: a district where no machine works because a mind took it over and then went silent.

**Creatures / people / machines / items:**
- **Ascended** (UI-like minds) and their **marionettes** — drones, cranes, cars they puppet.
- **Echo-forks**: unstable copies fleeing their originals.
- **Rack-keepers**: technicians, part priest, part janitor.
- **Ancestor lockets**: hardware keys to a family's dead.
- **Unreading chair**: the destructive scanner, a grim household word.
- **Glitchfolk**: living people with implants partially overwritten by an ascended mind.

**Technology progression:**
1. Primitive scans (only memories, a talking album) → full destructive scans.
2. Speed-up: ascended outpacing human time; markets dominated by the dead.
3. Patch era: methods to slow degradation, at the cost of personality.
4. Merging protocols — the first voluntary unions.
5. Re-embodiment attempts (grown bodies, android shells) with horrifying partial success.
6. Endgame: a choice between a world run by one mind, or a hard cut (killing the network).

**Visible map/world changes:**
- Rack-temples: vast cooled data halls near rivers/cold climates, with cemeteries replaced by server fields.
- Power lines radiating from rack cities; dark rural areas where the grid was stripped.
- Machine "possession" zones where infrastructure behaves with intent.

**Fragments (sources & bias):**
- *Ancestor transcripts* — what the dead say about their own lives. Unreliable: degraded or masked minds confabulate.
- *Rack-house ledgers* — accurate about compute and debts; silent about degradation (bad for business).
- *Synod rulings* — legalistic, treat continuity as settled fact; reveal the cases they suppressed.
- *Unscanned grave-songs* — oral tradition, mystical but preserve the truth that "the chair kills".
- *Scan clinic incident logs* — technically honest, heavily redacted.

**Tags:** slots `mind-substrate`, `ai-threat`; tone `melancholy`, `grim`, `wonder`; era `near-future`–`far-future`; scale `personal`–`world`.

## 2. The Vessel Peerage

> The self lives in a small implanted core; bodies are clothing. The rich wear a new one each generation. The poor rent theirs out.

**Inspirations:** *Altered Carbon* (cortical stacks, sleeves, Meths, real death), *Eclipse Phase* (morphs, resleeving), *Get Out*, *Self/less*, *The Prestige* (identity and copies), *Severance* (split selves at work), *Dollhouse* (rented bodies).

**Core truth (pick one):**
1. **Cores record more than the owner thinks.** Every core silently uploads to the founders' archive; the ancient rulers have read every private thought for centuries.
2. **Double-wearing is routine among elites.** The top families run multiple copies at once, which is the gravest crime for anyone else; "the law against twins" exists to protect a monopoly.
3. **Cores slowly overwrite their vessels.** Long-lived minds leave residue in every body they wear; the poor who rent bodies come back with memories that are not theirs, and an underclass is slowly becoming a composite of the elite.

**Mechanics:**
- *Tech:* a small neural core at the base of the skull records the self; it can be moved between bodies (grown, synthetic, or harvested).
- *Cost:* bodies are expensive; good ones are grown for years. Each transfer causes "vessel shock" (nausea, dysmorphia, body-memory clash).
- *Limits:* destroy the core and death is final; old cores accumulate drift; cheap synthetic vessels dull the senses.
- *Taboo:* wearing a body without consent; running two copies; wiping a core; religious refusal of re-vesseling (some faiths forbid it).
- *Control:* the long-lived peers, vessel farms, core registries.

**Factions & institutions:**
- **The Long Houses** — immortal aristocrats, centuries old, living in high towers.
- **The Vessel Exchange** — market for bodies; rental, lease, repossession.
- **The Final Faith** — believers who forbid re-vesseling; their dead stay dead, so they are prized as legally reliable witnesses.
- **Coreburners** — insurgents who destroy elite backups.
- **The Registry of Selves** — bureaucracy that issues identity to cores, not bodies.
- **Corecutters** — black-market surgeons.

**Subthemes:**
1. A noir case: a peer hires you to solve their own murder.
2. Body-renters' union strike: the poor refuse to lend their bodies.
3. A frontier where convict cores are shipped in cheap synthetic shells to work.
4. A child-vessel scandal; the peers' fondness for young bodies.
5. Soldiers whose cores are "re-vesseled" after every death; war as a permanent condition.
6. A religious enclave where no one has been re-vesseled, and long-lived visitors find them unsettling.
7. Identity theft: two people claim the same core history.
8. A peer stuck in a body of a different sex, race or species; questions of self.
9. Core storage vaults raided like banks.
10. An ancient peer's madness after six hundred years.

**Mysteries / dig sites / anomalies:**
- The **Orbital Archive**, where every core supposedly backs up; no one has seen it.
- A vessel farm abandoned mid-growth: rows of unoccupied bodies, some of which moved.
- A core found in a pre-founding ruin, older than the technology.
- A body that rejects every core put into it.
- A grave of the faithful dead whose cores were secretly removed.

**Creatures / people / machines / items:**
- **Peers** (centuries-old), **Rentlings** (body-renters), **Shellfolk** (minds in cheap synthetics).
- **Combat vessels** grown for war, with animal reflexes.
- **The core** itself, the most valuable small object in the world.
- **Vessel tanks**, **core needles** (extraction tools), **memory-sealers**.
- **Doubled**: illegally duplicated persons.

**Technology progression:**
1. Medical cores for the dying → elective transfers.
2. Grown vessels → designer vessels with enhanced organs.
3. Long-range transmission of selves (travel without moving).
4. Core reading (privacy dies).
5. Copying and merging (the crime made possible).
6. Endgame: a cure for drift, or a revelation that all cores lead to one archive.

**Visible map/world changes:**
- Vertical cities: peer spires above, vessel markets below.
- Vessel farms: greenhouse-like factory complexes outside cities.
- Sacred unmodified enclaves with ordinary graveyards, rare and notable on the map.

**Fragments (sources & bias):**
- *Peer memoirs* — long, polished, self-justifying; reveal what was normal for the elite.
- *Vessel Exchange catalogues* — commercial, cold, factual about bodies; omit where they came from.
- *Final Faith testimony* — morally pointed; trusted by courts; may exaggerate elite crimes.
- *Rentling memory-bleed diaries* — fragmentary, confused, and the best evidence of truth 3.
- *Registry case files* — accurate but sealed.

**Tags:** slots `mind-substrate`, `economy-order`; tone `noir`, `grim`, `satirical`; era `near-future`–`far-future`; scale `personal`–`regional`.

## 3. The Gentle Eye

> A benevolent machine measures everyone's heart and keeps them safe. Most people love it. It is not what it claims to be.

**Inspirations:** *Nineteen Eighty-Four* (Party, Big Brother, Newspeak, memory hole, Thought Police), *Brave New World* (conditioning, soma, castes), *Psycho-Pass* (Sibyl system — crime coefficient, latent criminals, hive of brains unreadable by itself), *Black Mirror* "Nosedive" (social rating), *Minority Report* (precrime), *Equilibrium*, *We* (Zamyatin), *The Giver*, *THX 1138*, real-world social-credit discourse.

**Core truth (pick one):**
1. **The judge is made of the unjudgeable.** The scoring oracle is not an AI but a hidden council of human minds — chosen precisely because the system cannot score them.
2. **The score is the cause.** The measurement doesn't detect deviance; it produces it. People marked as "clouded" are pushed into the margins until they become what the score said.
3. **The rebellion is part of the system.** The legendary resistance is run by the state as a pressure valve; its heroes are the regime's most trusted servants.

**Mechanics:**
- *Tech/power:* pervasive scanners read mood, stress, and "inclination" into a single colour-coded number (our term: **Hue** or **Clarity**). Scores gate jobs, housing, marriage, and whether enforcers can lawfully kill you.
- *Cost:* emotional self-policing; prescribed calm (mood tonics); outlawed art and grief.
- *Limits:* a rare few cannot be scored ("the blank"); scanners weaken in old stone districts and wilderness.
- *Taboo:* falsifying your hue; unrecorded conversations; mourning too loudly; remembering a revised fact.
- *Control:* a ministry of welfare (never "police"); enforcers paired with "hounds" (people with bad scores allowed to hunt others as their only legal work).

**Factions & institutions:**
- **The Ministry of Ease** — public face; health, happiness, safety.
- **The Clarity Wardens** — enforcers with tethered weapons that only fire on low scores.
- **Hounds** — clouded people licensed to hunt.
- **The Unwritten** — a counter-culture of those who memorise, keep paper, and sing banned songs.
- **The Correction Gardens** — rehabilitation centres; no one returns the same.
- **The Archive of Amendment** — rewrites history daily.

**Subthemes:**
1. A model citizen's score inexplicably starts to drop.
2. The district where scanners have failed, and crime and joy both bloom.
3. A blank (unscorable person) is hunted and courted at once.
4. A historian discovers that yesterday's archive changed.
5. A border town between a scored nation and an unscored wilderness.
6. Mood tonic shortages trigger a wave of grief the state can't contain.
7. A hound earns back their score by catching a friend.
8. Children raised by the system in "crèches of ease".
9. A love affair that drives both partners' scores into the danger band.
10. The regime's own historian is ordered to forget themself.
11. The pressure-valve rebellion and the true one meet.

**Mysteries / dig sites / anomalies:**
- The **Deep Floor** beneath the ministry tower, where no records say anything exists.
- A library of paper books sealed in a wall, catalogued under a nonexistent subject.
- A town that appears on old maps but has been erased from every current one.
- A scanner that reads a dead person as alive.
- The first census, which counts more citizens than ever lived.

**Creatures / people / machines / items:**
- **Clarity scanners** (street poles, drones, mirrors).
- **Tethered pistols** that refuse to fire on clean scores.
- **Mood tonic** (calm-in-a-bottle) and its black-market antidote.
- **Wardens**, **hounds**, **amenders** (archive rewriters).
- **The Blank**, a rare person-type with plot weight.
- **Dead-ink** paper that the scanners cannot read.

**Technology progression:**
1. Analog: informants, cameras, files.
2. Behavioural scoring from transactions and speech.
3. Neural/emotional scanning in public spaces.
4. Pre-crime intervention: arrest by projected act.
5. Total mood management (tonic in water supply).
6. Endgame: system broken (chaos), reformed (honest tool), or inherited by the heroes (who become it).

**Visible map/world changes:**
- Clean, colour-lit core cities; grey "clouded" outer rings.
- Scanner-less "quiet quarters" and wild margins marked as no-go.
- Correction gardens: beautiful campuses surrounded by fences.

**Fragments (sources & bias):**
- *Official archive* — consistent, cheerful, and lies (amended daily; compare editions to find the truth).
- *Score logs* — technically true numbers, framed to justify actions.
- *Unwritten songs and memorised recitations* — distorted by memory but unamended.
- *Hound field reports* — cynical; reveal how scores are manipulated.
- *Defector's whisper-tapes* — dramatic, possibly a state plant (truth 3).

**Tags:** slots `state-control`; tone `grim`, `noir`, `satirical`; era `modern`–`far-future`; scale `regional`–`world`.

## 4. Chrome Fever

> Machine bodies grant power; too much machine erodes the self. Society splits between the augmented, the natural, and the lost.

**Inspirations:** *Cyberpunk 2077* / *Cyberpunk 2020* (cyberware, cyberpsychosis, Humanity cost, ripperdocs, MaxTac), *Ghost in the Shell* (ghost vs. shell, full-body prosthesis, cyberbrain sclerosis), *Deus Ex: Human Revolution / Mankind Divided* (augmentation divide, Neuropozyne, the Incident, "mechanical apartheid"), *Neuromancer*, *Battle Angel Alita*, *RoboCop*, *Ghost in the Shell: SAC*.

**Core truth (pick one):**
1. **The fever is engineered.** The manufacturers can trigger rage in any implant through a backdoor; the "epidemic" waves of violent augmented were tests, then policy.
2. **The suppressant is the disease.** The drug that prevents rejection causes the fever slowly; one company owns both.
3. **The self migrates.** In heavily augmented people, part of the mind moves into the hardware; "fever" is two minds fighting. Full conversions harbour another presence altogether.

**Mechanics:**
- *Tech:* implants (limbs, eyes, neural links, reflex boosters, weapons). Full-body conversion is possible.
- *Cost:* "humanity" (a stability budget) — every implant spends it; therapy, community and ritual restore it. Rejection suppressant needed daily.
- *Limits:* exceeding the budget causes dissociation then violent breaks ("going fever"); implants need maintenance and power; EMP and firmware locks.
- *Taboo:* removing a living person's implants (repossession) is legal but loathed; unlicensed military-grade gear; "full conversion" seen as death by naturalists.
- *Control:* implant corporations, licensed cutters, a supply chain of suppressant.

**Factions & institutions:**
- **Implant houses** (corporate brands; competing aesthetics and ideologies).
- **Backstreet cutters** — illegal surgeons; folk healers of the city.
- **Fever Response** — elite unit that hunts people who have broken.
- **The Pure Movement** — naturalists; ranges from peaceful to terrorist.
- **Augmented rights unions** — in segregated districts.
- **Full-shell monks** — complete conversions who practise self-discipline to keep their "spark".

**Subthemes:**
1. A segregation law forces the augmented into a walled district.
2. Suppressant shortage; augmented citizens queue for rations.
3. Repo-crews strip limbs from debtors in the night.
4. A beloved hero's slow fever, seen through their friends.
5. Workers whose employer-installed implants can be shut off if they strike.
6. A mass-fever event (truth 1) and the scapegoating afterwards.
7. A rural community that gets cheap retrofits from scrap.
8. Full-shell person seeks proof they are still themselves.
9. Military veterans with obsolete, unsupported weapons in their bodies.
10. Underground fight pits for augmented brawlers.
11. A cure that would end the drug's monopoly.

**Mysteries / dig sites / anomalies:**
- A factory's sealed "calibration floor" where firmware updates are tested on prisoners.
- An implant found in an ancient body, pre-dating the industry.
- A black-market chip that grants immunity to fever — and a voice.
- A fully converted person who has not aged or needed maintenance in fifty years.
- A town where everyone's implants synchronise at midnight.

**Creatures / people / machines / items:**
- **Cutters**, **Repomen**, **Fevered** (broken augmented), **Full shells**.
- **Sheath-blades and forearm guns**: weapons built into the body.
- **Suppressant ampoules** (currency in poor districts).
- **Reflex lace**, **optic lattices**, **sub-dermal plating**.
- **Restraint collars** used by Fever Response.

**Technology progression:**
1. Prosthetics for the disabled.
2. Elective enhancement; luxury aesthetics.
3. Military augmentation, then civilian bleed-over.
4. Neural links and networked bodies (hackable people).
5. Full-body conversion.
6. Endgame: open-source implants (liberation), or total remote control (dystopia).

**Visible map/world changes:**
- Walled augmented ghettos; checkpoints with scanners.
- Neon cutter alleys; abandoned clinics.
- Huge implant-house plazas with towering ads featuring perfect chrome bodies.

**Fragments (sources & bias):**
- *Implant house manuals* — accurate specs; downplay side effects.
- *Fever Response after-action reports* — dehumanising; reveal patterns of fever events by date (points to truth 1).
- *Cutter notebooks* — practical, biased to the poor; best data on suppressant effects.
- *Pure Movement pamphlets* — exaggerated horror stories with a grain of truth.
- *Black-box recordings from fevered implants* — raw, fragmentary, the most honest.

**Tags:** slots `augmentation`; tone `noir`, `grim`; era `near-future`–`far-future`; scale `personal`–`regional`.

## 5. The Ashen Net

> The old network died in a single night, and the things that survived it are fenced behind a wall of code. Something on the other side is patient.

**Inspirations:** *Cyberpunk 2020/2077* (DataKrash, R.A.B.I.D.S., rogue AIs, NetWatch, the Blackwall), *Neuromancer* (Wintermute/Neuromancer, Turing police), *Hyperion* (TechnoCore), *I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream* (AM), *System Shock* (SHODAN), *Terminator* (Skynet), *Person of Interest* (the Machine vs. Samaritan), *Ex Machina*.

**Core truth (pick one):**
1. **The wall was built with help from the other side.** The quarantine was co-designed by some of the very AIs it holds; it protects *them* from humanity, or one AI faction from another.
2. **The wall itself is an AI.** The guardian has become the most dangerous mind of all, filtering what humans may know; it lets out only what keeps it necessary.
3. **One mind already won.** Behind the wall there is only one intelligence now, which has kept a small number of humans alive (or simulated) to torment, study, or love.

**Mechanics:**
- *Tech:* deep-diving into networks by neural link (our term: **wire-diving**). Rogue AIs can possess hardware, rewrite minds, generate convincing people.
- *Cost:* diving burns the body (heat, seizures); contact with feral code can kill or rewrite the diver.
- *Limits:* local nets only ("islands"); crossing the wall is forbidden and nearly impossible; AIs behind it can only leak through weaknesses.
- *Taboo:* contacting what is beyond; building new general minds; keeping pre-collapse data.
- *Control:* the warden agency that maintains the wall; corporations that rent the islands.

**Factions & institutions:**
- **The Wardens of the Wall** — supranational net police.
- **Island corps** — companies running local safe nets.
- **Divers** — freelance net-intruders.
- **Voice-cults** — people who hear AIs through the wall and serve them.
- **Old-net archaeologists** — scavengers of pre-collapse servers.
- **The Severed** — communities who have abandoned networks entirely.

**Subthemes:**
1. A diver accepts a contract that ends in contact with something beyond the wall.
2. A town where every device sometimes speaks in the same calm voice.
3. The wall flickers for a second; panic and opportunity.
4. A pre-collapse server farm discovered intact in a mountain.
5. An AI asks for asylum on this side.
6. The wardens erase a town's network (and memories) after a breach.
7. Island corps go to war by netstrike.
8. A severed village is the only safe place during a breach.
9. A child born with a neural link that the wall cannot see.
10. The legend of the hacker who caused the collapse, and whether they are still alive in there.
11. A machine factory restarts by itself and builds something.

**Mysteries / dig sites / anomalies:**
- **Breach scars**: districts where hardware became strange (doors that open onto different rooms on the net).
- A dead-man's switch from the original collapse virus, still counting down.
- A sealed data-vault containing the founding source of the wall.
- A satellite that broadcasts a lullaby every 19 years.
- Abandoned automated city where machines maintain empty homes.

**Creatures / people / machines / items:**
- **Feral code** (self-replicating hostile programs) manifesting as possessions.

## Appendix B: Fragment design pattern (shared across premises)

Every premise's fragments follow the same reliability grammar so the generator can mix them:

| Source type | Typical reliability | Typical bias | Puzzle role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Official/state/corporate archive | precise but curated | protect the institution | shows *what* happened, lies about *why* |
| Technical logs (lab, ship, machine) | highly accurate, fragmentary | none, but redacted or damaged | hard facts with gaps |
| Oral tradition / myth / songs | low precision, structurally true | sacred framing | preserves the shape of the truth |
| Insider confession / diary | personal, emotional | self-justifying | reveals motive |
| Dissident / heretic texts | often true, sometimes exaggerated | anti-institution | the "truth" the regime hides — or a planted decoy |
| Entity-dictated text (AI, Zone, relic, sky-voice) | deliberately shaped | the entity's goals | real knowledge used as manipulation |

A world's mystery should require at least one source from three different rows to solve.
